# Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) Reference Guide

## Overview
Thyroid function evaluation assesses the endocrine regulation of cellular basal metabolic rate via the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis.

## Key Parameters & Clinical Context

### Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)
- **Biological Role**: Glycoprotein hormone secreted by the anterior pituitary gland to stimulate thyroid follicular cells to synthesize and release triiodothyronine (T3) and thyroxine (T4).
- **Reference Range**: Commonly 0.4–4.0 µIU/mL (or mIU/L), though specific laboratory methods and trimester-specific obstetric guidelines may differ.
- **Contextual Note**: Serum TSH levels exhibit an inverse logarithmic relationship with circulating free thyroxine. Values above reported reference ranges prompt evaluation of primary hypothyroid states, whereas values below suggest hyperthyroidism or pituitary dysfunction.

### Free Thyroxine (Free T4)
- **Biological Role**: Unbound, biologically active fraction of the primary hormone synthesized by the thyroid gland.
- **Reference Range**: Typically 0.8–1.8 ng/dL.
- **Contextual Note**: Interpreted conjointly with TSH to categorize subclinical vs overt thyroid functional variations.

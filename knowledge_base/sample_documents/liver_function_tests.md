# Liver Function & Hepatic Panel Reference Guide

## Overview
Liver function tests (LFTs) comprise enzymes, proteins, and clearance products reflecting hepatocellular integrity, biliary ductal patency, and synthetic capacity.

## Key Parameters & Clinical Context

### Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT)
- **Biological Role**: Intracellular transaminase enzyme located predominantly in hepatocyte cytoplasm.
- **Reference Range**: Typically 7–56 U/L (laboratory cutoffs vary).
- **Contextual Note**: Serum release occurs when hepatocyte membranes undergo stress or necrosis. Mild elevations can be non-specific, transient, or linked to fatty liver, medications, or viral infections.

### Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST)
- **Biological Role**: Transaminase found in hepatocytes as well as cardiac muscle, skeletal muscle, and erythrocytes.
- **Reference Range**: Typically 10–40 U/L.
- **Contextual Note**: The AST/ALT ratio is frequently observed by clinicians to distinguish alcoholic liver injury from other etiologies.

### Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)
- **Biological Role**: Enzyme concentrated in biliary canalicular membranes, bone, placenta, and intestinal mucosa.
- **Reference Range**: Commonly 44–147 U/L.
- **Contextual Note**: Elevation commonly accompanies biliary tract obstruction or increased bone turnover.

### Total Bilirubin
- **Biological Role**: Breakdown product of heme catabolism cleared via glucuronidation by hepatocytes and excreted into bile.
- **Reference Range**: Typically 0.2–1.2 mg/dL.
- **Contextual Note**: Jaundice becomes clinically noticeable when total bilirubin exceeds ~2.5–3.0 mg/dL.

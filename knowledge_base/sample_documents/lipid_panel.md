# Lipid Panel Reference Guide

## Overview
A lipid panel quantifies blood lipids and lipoprotein fractions to assess circulating cholesterol and triglyceride distributions.

## Key Parameters & Clinical Context

### Total Cholesterol
- **Biological Role**: Essential sterol molecule necessary for membrane fluidity, steroid hormone biosynthesis, and bile acid production.
- **Reference Range**: Desirable levels are generally reported as < 200 mg/dL.
- **Contextual Note**: Total cholesterol comprises HDL, LDL, and VLDL fractions. Elevated total cholesterol reflects combined lipid fractions and is assessed within cardiovascular risk calculators rather than in isolation.

### High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL) Cholesterol
- **Biological Role**: Facilitates reverse cholesterol transport from peripheral tissues back to hepatocytes.
- **Reference Range**: Typically > 40 mg/dL for men and > 50 mg/dL for women. Higher values are considered protective in epidemiological models.

### Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) Cholesterol
- **Biological Role**: Primary transporter of cholesterol to peripheral tissues; prone to vascular subendothelial deposition.
- **Reference Range**: Optimal targets are usually < 100 mg/dL, with individualized goals depending on personal cardiovascular risk strata.

### Serum Triglycerides
- **Biological Role**: Primary storage form of dietary and endogenous fatty acids.
- **Reference Range**: Normal fasting levels are typically < 150 mg/dL.
